It installs

A real icon set, a manifest, and the iOS tags most guides forget. Android offers “Install app”; iPhone gets a proper home-screen icon and opens with no address bar.

It survives losing signal

A network-first service worker keeps a copy of the shell. Lose the connection and the app still opens instead of the dinosaur — but online visitors always get the live version, so nothing ever goes stale.

It comes with proof

A one-page report listing every check, what it measured, and side-by-side screenshots of your app online and with the network disconnected. Yours to keep, or forward to whoever built the site.

Two things I would rather say up front

These nine are my own builds, not client work. I built the tooling and ran it across my own catalogue first — a game, a food-allergy scanner, a course finder, a store map, an AI assistant with four separate screens. That is why the numbers vary: each app got the checks it actually needed, not a fixed template.

Not every site should be an app. If your site is one page of text, a service worker adds a file and buys you almost nothing, and I will tell you so rather than take the job. This is worth real money when people come back to your site repeatedly, or use it somewhere with bad signal — a job site, a basement, a shop floor, a course, a parking garage.
